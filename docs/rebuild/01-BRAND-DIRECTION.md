# Brand Direction

**Working name:** Kiteb  
**Status:** Provisional; not cleared for public or commercial use

## Why the old identity is being retired

The previous identity was inconsistent across the repository and product. It mixed `aqlix-ai`, `Iraqi AI Chat System`, `Iraqi AI`, and `@iraqi-ai/*`, while the visible interface did not support the breadth of the stated product claims.

The rebuild requires one identity connected to a focused product promise rather than a broad list of technologies and cultural claims.

## Working name: Kiteb

Kiteb is a compact, memorable working name derived from the Arabic writing and document context. It supports the intended product territory without restricting the application to chat alone.

This name is a design and implementation placeholder until a professional clearance process covers:

- trademarks and company registrations in target markets;
- relevant domains and common misspellings;
- major social and app-store handles;
- Arabic pronunciation and transcription;
- conflicts in software, AI, productivity, document, and knowledge-management categories.

Package namespaces, repository names, production domains, and legal copy must not be permanently migrated until clearance is complete.

## Positioning

### Category

Arabic-first AI workspace.

### Audience

Arabic-speaking professionals and small teams who work with bilingual documents and conversations.

### Promise

Bring the context. Understand it. Turn it into work.

### Arabic promise

اجمع السياق، افهمه، وحوّله إلى عمل واضح.

### Differentiation

- Arabic, English, RTL, LTR, and mixed content are first-class product concerns.
- Documents and sources remain visible throughout the work.
- The product is organized around durable workspaces and outputs, not isolated prompts.
- Capability and uncertainty are presented honestly.

## Brand character

Kiteb should feel:

- calm rather than noisy;
- precise rather than grandiose;
- capable rather than futuristic for its own sake;
- local-aware without clichés or national symbolism as decoration;
- professional without becoming institutional or cold;
- bilingual without forcing mirrored copy into every surface.

## Voice principles

### Clear

Use direct language and concrete actions. Prefer “Add a document” over “Unlock multimodal intelligence.”

### Honest

Never describe planned or placeholder behavior as available. Avoid unsupported percentages and universal claims.

### Useful

Name the user outcome before the underlying technology.

### Bilingual by context

Use the language selected by the user. Mixed-language labels are reserved for onboarding, language controls, and places where they genuinely aid understanding.

### Respectful, not ceremonial

Default copy should be warm and professional. Cultural or religious phrasing should not be inserted automatically without a validated user preference and appropriate context.

## Visual direction

### Foundation

- Warm off-white canvas instead of generic pure white.
- Deep ink text with a controlled evergreen accent.
- Spacious layouts with strong reading hierarchy.
- Quiet grid and paper-like references used sparingly.
- Rounded surfaces with restrained depth, not glass-heavy dashboards.
- Motion explains state changes; it is not background decoration.

### Typography

- Arabic: Noto Sans Arabic for interface text and Cairo for selected display use.
- English: a neutral system or modern grotesk face.
- Mixed content must preserve natural shaping, punctuation, numbers, and isolation.
- Formal Arabic display typography is optional and should not reduce interface readability.

### Interface motifs

- Context stack: question, sources, and draft shown as connected layers.
- Source markers: subtle numbered references with direct inspection.
- Workspace rail: persistent, quiet navigation rather than an analytics-heavy dashboard.
- Draft canvas: output is treated as editable work, not only a chat bubble.

## Logo direction

The first implementation uses a typographic wordmark only. A permanent symbol is deferred until the name is cleared.

Avoid:

- generic spark, robot, brain, chat-bubble, or magic-wand marks;
- flag motifs;
- ornamental Arabic calligraphy that harms small-size readability;
- gradients used as the primary identity;
- implying certification, authority, or government affiliation.

## Product-copy guardrails

Do not publish claims such as:

- “production ready”;
- “bank-grade security”;
- “100% compliant”;
- “verified Islamic compliance”;
- measured dialect, accuracy, uptime, response-time, or coverage percentages;
- native payment support before live provider integration and verification.

Claims enter public copy only with an owner, measurement method, date, environment, and reproducible evidence.
